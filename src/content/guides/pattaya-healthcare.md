Pattaya has better medical care than most visitors expect, and the gap between the options is wider than anywhere else you are likely to have lived. A private hospital here can be faster and more comfortable than what you are used to at home. A government hospital costs a fraction of that and works very differently.

This guide is written from using them. Last checked **4 September 2026**.

## In an Emergency

**1669** is Thailand's national emergency medical number. **1155** reaches the Tourist Police, who speak English and can help if you are stuck.

For anything serious, go to a private hospital emergency department rather than waiting for a decision. Bangkok Hospital Pattaya and Pattaya Memorial both run 24-hour emergency departments and will treat you first and sort out payment afterwards.

If you can walk and it is not urgent, read on before choosing.

## Bangkok Hospital Pattaya

This is the one we use and the one we recommend without hesitation.

It is JCI-accredited, has been running since 1990 as part of the BDMS group, and functions as the main referral hospital for Thailand's Eastern Seaboard — roughly 300 beds, over 100 full-time specialists, and departments for cardiology, neuroscience, orthopaedics and surgery.

**What a visit is actually like.** Registration needs your passport and takes a few minutes. After a short triage check we were seen within minutes — not the hour or more that phrase usually means. A **German-speaking interpreter** sat in and translated the whole consultation. The hospital keeps interpreters for more than 20 languages on staff, so this is a standing service rather than luck.

**The bill came in lower than expected.** That is the honest version — we are not going to quote a figure, because what you pay depends entirely on what is wrong with you. But the mental price tag most Europeans arrive with is set by private medicine at home, and this was well under it.

The hospital also runs an Expat Care Center that handles appointments and paperwork, and a hotel call service where a doctor visits your room.

Who it is not for: if you are uninsured and the problem is minor, you are paying a premium for speed and language you may not need.

## The Other Hospitals

Pattaya has six hospitals — two government, four private.

**Pattaya Memorial Hospital** is the city's oldest private hospital, over thirty years in service, and sits in the middle: cheaper than Bangkok Hospital, faster and more comfortable than the government route. A sensible default for something that needs a doctor but not a specialist.

**Pattaya International Hospital** and **Jomtien Hospital** are the other private options, both smaller.

**Banglamung Hospital** and **Pattaya City Hospital** are government-run. They are dramatically cheaper and they are where most Thais go. Expect long waits, less English, and be aware that foreigners are sometimes charged above the local rate. For a straightforward problem and a tight budget, they work.

The practical rule: government hospitals for cheap and slow, Memorial for the middle, Bangkok Hospital when it matters or when you need to be understood precisely.

## Blood Tests and Lab Work

This is where Pattaya is genuinely unusual. The city is full of private labs, walk-in testing is normal, and a panel that would take a referral and three weeks at home is done the same day.

Two we have used, with different outcomes.

**Topform Clinic** (Central Pattaya) does IV vitamin drips and blood work. The vitamin C infusion was fine. The blood panel was not handled well: they ran a **testosterone test at 3pm**. Testosterone follows a daily rhythm and peaks in the morning — the standard is to draw between 8 and 10am, and an afternoon reading on the same person can come back meaningfully lower. Nobody mentioned this, and a clinic selling that test should be the one to raise it. The result was not worth much.

**M.T. Inter Medical Laboratory Clinic** (South Pattaya) did the same work properly. A full panel — total and free testosterone, DHT and the surrounding markers — with results back within a few days and no complications. The place does not look like a polished international clinic, and that turned out not to matter at all. Judge labs on what comes back, not on the waiting room.

**If you are testing hormones, book the morning slot yourself.** Do not assume the clinic will schedule around the biology. That single detail was the difference between the two visits above.

A word on what to do with the numbers: getting tested here is easy and cheap, and that is a genuine advantage of living in Thailand. Acting on the results is a separate question, and hormone therapy without a doctor monitoring you is not something a travel guide should be talking anyone into. Take the panel to a physician.

## Pharmacies

Thai pharmacies sell a lot of things over the counter that need a prescription in Europe or North America — antibiotics, antifungals, blood pressure medication and more.

Two things are worth understanding about that.

**Available does not mean unregulated.** Antibiotics formally require a doctor's authorisation. Smaller independent pharmacies often do not enforce it; chain pharmacies like Boots and Watsons, and hospital pharmacies, generally do. The rules exist, enforcement varies by counter.

**The rules move.** Oral tramadol was reclassified as a specially controlled medicine after a July 2025 announcement, and since early 2026 it needs a prescription. If you relied on being able to buy something here previously, check again rather than assuming.

Narcotics, psychotropics and strong painkillers are controlled or outright illegal, and the fact that a small shop is willing to sell something does not make buying it legal. That distinction matters more in Thailand than most places.

**Bringing your own medication in:** carry it in the original packaging with the prescription. Some drugs that are routine at home are restricted here — check before you fly rather than at the airport.

## Insurance

Private treatment in Pattaya is affordable by European standards and still expensive if you have no cover and something goes properly wrong. A serious accident, a few nights in a private hospital and a repatriation flight is a bill that ends people financially.

Two things to check in your policy rather than assume:

- **Intoxication exclusions.** Many travel policies decline claims for injuries sustained while drunk. In a city with Pattaya's nightlife, this is not a theoretical clause.
- **Motorbike cover.** Most policies require a licence valid for the bike you are riding. Renting a scooter on a car licence, which is common here, can void the cover entirely.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Which hospital should I go to in Pattaya?

Bangkok Hospital Pattaya for anything serious or where being understood precisely matters — it is JCI-accredited and keeps interpreters for over 20 languages. Pattaya Memorial for the middle ground. Banglamung or Pattaya City Hospital if cost is the deciding factor and you can wait.

### Do doctors in Pattaya speak English?

At the private hospitals, yes. Bangkok Hospital Pattaya also provides interpreters in more than 20 languages including German, which is a staffed service rather than something you have to arrange.

### Can I get blood tests without a referral?

Yes. Walk-in lab testing is normal in Pattaya and results usually come back within days. Book hormone panels for the morning — testosterone in particular should be drawn between 8 and 10am.

### Can I buy antibiotics without a prescription?

Often, at independent pharmacies. Formally they require a doctor's authorisation, and chain and hospital pharmacies tend to enforce it. Availability is not the same as it being a good idea.

### What is the emergency number in Thailand?

1669 for medical emergencies, 1155 for the Tourist Police.

### Is travel insurance worth it for Thailand?

Yes, and read the exclusions. Intoxication-related injury and riding a motorbike without the correct licence are the two clauses that most often void a claim here.
