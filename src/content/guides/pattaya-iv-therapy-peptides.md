Pattaya has a fast-growing market for IV drips, NAD+ infusions and peptide protocols. Some of it is ordinary wellness. Some of it involves compounds that no drug regulator anywhere has approved, sold with language that sounds like it means the opposite.

This page does not recommend a provider. We have not used any of them, and recommending injectable treatment on the strength of a website would be irresponsible. What it does is lay out what is on offer, where the regulatory status actually stands as of **4 September 2026**, and what to ask before you hand over a credit card.

## IV Drips

This is the uncontroversial end of the market. Saline, vitamins, sometimes NAD+, administered in a lounge or delivered to your hotel. It is the same product sold in London and Los Angeles, at Thai prices.

**RejuveHub** on South Pattaya Road is the most visible operator — a drip lounge with recliner pods, prices starting around 999 THB, roughly 45-minute sessions, and hotel or villa visits for about 500 THB extra. They also offer LED light therapy and a range of wellness services, and take bookings by WhatsApp.

Their site describes treatments as nurse-administered by licensed nurses. For vitamin infusions that is normal and fine.

**What to know generally:** the evidence for IV vitamin therapy in healthy people is thin. If you are dehydrated after three nights out, saline will help, and it would have helped just as much from a hospital. The vitamin content is mostly doing less than the marketing suggests. That is not a reason to avoid it — it is a reason not to pay a premium expecting a medical effect.

## Peptide Protocols

This is where it gets more complicated, and where the language on provider websites does a lot of work.

Two models operate in Pattaya. **Clinic-based programmes**, like the one- to six-month peptide courses RejuveHub sells, with a consultation included and dosing described as tailored to your bloodwork. And **remote prescribing services** such as PeptidesThailand, which run a video consultation with a physician registered with the Medical Council of Thailand, then deliver to your door in two to three days with a certificate of analysis for the batch.

The remote model is structurally better on one point: it names a licensed physician. RejuveHub's material names registered nurses but no doctor, which is a gap worth asking about before starting a multi-month course of anything injectable.

But the more important question is not who hands it over. It is what is in it.

## Where the Approval Status Actually Stands

Provider sites use the phrase **"clinical-grade"** heavily. It is not a regulatory category. It means the substance was mixed in a laboratory. It does not mean approved, tested for safety in humans, or evaluated by anyone.

Here is the actual position for compounds commonly sold in Pattaya:

**Retatrutide** is the one that should stop you. It is an Eli Lilly compound still in **Phase 3 trials** — the TRIUMPH programme, with over 5,800 participants, is running right now. It is not approved anywhere in the world. A compounding lab producing it is manufacturing a drug whose safety evaluation has not finished. Whatever you think of the science, you would be taking it outside the trial that exists to establish whether it is safe.

**BPC-157 and TB-500** sit differently, and it is only fair to say so. Their status is moving: the FDA removed both from Category 2 in April 2026, and on 23 July 2026 its Pharmacy Compounding Advisory Committee voted 8–6 to recommend BPC-157 for compounding. That is a real shift. But neither is an approved drug, neither has a recognised USP monograph, and the FDA has issued no final decision. "Under active reconsideration" is not the same as "cleared".

**Epitalon, GHK-Cu and Semax** are not approved as medicines in the EU, UK or US. Semax is licensed in Russia. Human evidence for the others is limited.

None of that makes these compounds automatically dangerous. It makes them unevaluated, which is a different thing, and it means the safety net you are used to is not there.

## The Seven Questions

If you are going to do this anyway — and plenty of people will — these are worth asking before the first injection, in writing where possible.

1. **Is this compound approved as a medicine anywhere?** If the answer is a brand story rather than a jurisdiction, that is your answer.
2. **Which named, licensed doctor is prescribing?** Not "our medical team". A name and a registration number you can check with the Medical Council of Thailand.
3. **Is that doctor reachable if something goes wrong at 2am?** A video consultation months ago is not clinical supervision.
4. **Who issued the certificate of analysis, and can I see it?** A COA from the lab that made the product is a self-assessment. An independent one is worth something.
5. **What happens if I have a reaction?** Which hospital, who pays, who is liable. Get this before you need it.
6. **Does my insurance cover complications?** Almost certainly not. Travel and health policies routinely exclude elective treatment and anything involving unapproved substances. Assume you are self-funding any complication, and price that in.
7. **What am I actually allowed to take home?** See below.

## The "Travel Certificate" Is Not a Free Pass

At least one Pattaya provider advertises a medical certificate to support international travel with prescribed peptides, presented as a feature.

Treat it as a risk that has been dressed up. A Thai certificate documents a Thai prescription. It does not make an unapproved substance legal to import into Germany, the UK, Australia or anywhere else, and customs authorities are not obliged to accept it. If you are carrying anything across a border, check the rules of the country you are entering, not the one you are leaving.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Are peptides legal in Thailand?

A licensed Thai physician can prescribe compounded preparations, which is the basis these services operate on. That is a different question from whether the compound is an approved medicine — mostly it is not — and a different question again from whether you can legally take it home.

### Is "clinical-grade" a real standard?

No. It is marketing. It indicates the substance was produced in a laboratory, nothing more. Look for approval status and an independent certificate of analysis instead.

### Will my travel insurance cover a complication?

Almost certainly not. Elective treatment and unapproved substances are standard exclusions. Assume you are paying for any complication yourself.

### Can I take prescribed peptides home?

Check the import rules of your destination country before you fly. A Thai prescription and certificate do not override them, whatever the provider's website implies.

### Where can I get blood work done first?

Walk-in labs are common in Pattaya and results come back within days. Our [healthcare guide](/guides/pattaya-healthcare/) covers two we have used, including which one handled hormone testing properly.
