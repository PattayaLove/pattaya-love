Muay Thai is one of the few reasons people move to Thailand rather than visit it. Pattaya has been a training destination for decades, and since 2024 there has been a second reason to train here: it is one of the activities that qualifies for a five-year visa.

That combination has produced a market worth understanding before you spend money on either side of it.

Last checked **4 September 2026**. Visa rules change and two requirements changed four days before this was written — verify anything decision-critical with a Thai embassy or a licensed visa agent rather than with us.

## The DTV, and Why Muay Thai Is On the List

The **Destination Thailand Visa** was introduced in 2024. It is a five-year multiple-entry visa granting 180 days per entry, extendable once by another 180 days inside the country. For anyone who wants to spend long stretches in Thailand without visa runs, it is the most useful thing the government has issued in years.

It covers remote workers and, separately, a set of **soft power activities**. Muay Thai training is on that list. Thai cooking courses and medical treatment are too. General language schools are not.

**The core requirements:**

- **500,000 THB in liquid funds**, roughly 17,000 USD, evidenced by six months of bank statements with the balance held for at least the last three. Cryptocurrency, stocks and investment portfolios are not accepted — it has to be cash you could withdraw today.
- **An acceptance letter from the gym**, naming you, your passport number, your nationality and your training dates, addressed to the specific embassy you are applying to. Gyms supply their business licence and the director's ID alongside it.
- **You must apply from outside Thailand.** Applications are not accepted in-country.

**Two requirements were added on 31 August 2026:**

- **Proof of permanent residence**, replacing the looser evidence of your current location that a hotel booking used to satisfy.
- **A criminal record certificate** from your home country.

If you researched the DTV earlier this year, your information is out of date. Budget time for a police certificate, which in some countries takes weeks.

## The Package Problem

A market has grown up selling the letter rather than the training. One gym advertises fifteen Muay Thai sessions spread across nine months for around 23,000 THB, with the DTV acceptance letter included.

Fifteen sessions over nine months is under two a month. Nobody buying that is training. They are buying a document, and the training is the wrapper it comes in.

We are not going to tell you this is illegal, because we do not know that and it is not our call. What we will say is that it looks exactly like the thing the August 2026 changes were written to catch. When a government adds a police certificate and a proof-of-residence requirement to a visa category, it is not because the category was working as intended. If your application rests on a letter from a gym you have barely attended, you are exposed to a rule change you cannot predict, on a visa you may have built a life around.

Train because you want to train. If the visa follows, that is a benefit and not a business model.

## The Gyms

We should be straight about this section: **we have not trained at these gyms.** What follows is what they are and what they charge, not a recommendation between them. Muay Thai gyms differ enormously in how they treat a beginner versus a serious fighter, and that is not something you can establish from a website.

**Fairtex Training Center** is the best known, headquartered in Bang Lamung and running since 1971. The training centre sits inside the Fairtex Hotel building, so accommodation and gym are the same address — convenient for a training camp, and the reason it is the default choice for people arriving alone. Food is not included with the rooms.

**Kombat Group** combines Muay Thai with functional fitness and weight-loss programmes and has a pool. A different proposition: closer to a fitness retreat that includes Muay Thai than a fight camp.

Pattaya has smaller camps beyond these two, and they are where the more interesting training often is. That is precisely the information a website cannot give you — go and watch a session before you pay for a month.

**Pattaya Boxing World** is worth naming separately because it is frequently listed as a gym and is primarily a **stadium**: 600 seats, fights every night from 20:00 to 23:00. Go to watch, not to train.

## What It Costs

Figures advertised by Pattaya gyms as of September 2026. Treat them as the shape of the market rather than a quote, and confirm directly.

| | Range |
|---|---|
| Single session | 400–800 THB |
| One month, training only | roughly 10,000–15,000 THB |
| Six-month training programme | 15,000–65,000 THB |
| Package with accommodation | 80,000 THB and up per month |

The spread on the six-month programmes tells you how different these products are. At the bottom end you are buying gym access. At the top you are buying coached training. The accommodation packages are a separate calculation again — compare them against renting a room yourself, because Pattaya rents are low and the convenience premium is real.

## Insurance, Which Most People Get Wrong

**Check whether your policy covers combat sports before your first session.** Many travel policies classify Muay Thai as a hazardous activity and exclude it, and some exclude contact sparring while covering training. An injury in a Pattaya gym is not exotic — it is the normal outcome of the sport over a long enough period.

Two of the insurers we link elsewhere on this site cover long stays, and both need reading on this specific point rather than assuming: see the insurance section of our [healthcare guide](/guides/pattaya-healthcare/), which also covers which hospital to use. Bangkok Hospital Pattaya is the one to know before you need it.

## Practical Notes

**Where to stay.** Gym accommodation is simplest for a first camp. After that, [East Pattaya](/areas/east-pattaya/) and [Soi Buakhao](/areas/soi-buakhao/) are where long-stay rents are lowest, and [Jomtien](/areas/jomtien/) if you want the beach. Our [where to stay overview](/stay/) covers the areas in detail.

**Getting around.** Most people rent a scooter. Read the [scooter guide](/guides/pattaya-scooter-rental/) first, particularly the licence section — riding without the right licence voids most insurance, which is a poor combination with a sport that already tests your cover.

**Timing.** Training through the hot season, March to May, is harder than it sounds. November to February is the comfortable window and also the busy one.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Does Muay Thai training qualify for the DTV visa?

Yes. It is one of the recognised soft power activities, alongside Thai cooking and medical treatment. You need an acceptance letter from a licensed gym, plus the financial and background requirements below.

### How much money do I need for the DTV?

500,000 THB in liquid funds, evidenced across six months of statements with the balance held for at least the last three. Crypto and investment portfolios do not count.

### What changed on 31 August 2026?

Two additions: proof of permanent residence, and a criminal record certificate from your home country. Guides published before that date are missing both.

### Can I apply for the DTV while in Thailand?

No. Applications must be made from outside the country.

### Are the cheap DTV training packages safe?

They are legal as far as we know, and they are also the obvious target of the tightening that has already begun. Buying a visa route rather than training is a risk you carry for five years.

### Does travel insurance cover Muay Thai?

Often not. Combat sports are a common exclusion and sparring is sometimes excluded separately from training. Check the wording before your first session.
