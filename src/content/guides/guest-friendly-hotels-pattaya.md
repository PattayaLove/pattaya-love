## What Guest-Friendly and Joiner Fee Actually Mean

A **joiner** is anyone who comes to your room who is not registered as a guest. A **joiner policy** is the hotel's rules for them. A **guest-friendly hotel** (also called girl-friendly or joiner-friendly) lets one joiner stay overnight at no charge. A **joiner fee** is what non-guest-friendly hotels charge for the same thing, typically 500 to 1,500 THB per person per night in Pattaya, though a few hotels quote more and a few simply refuse.

Most Pattaya hotels are, in practice, guest-friendly. The city's mid-range hotel stock was built for single male travellers and the owners are not naive about why people come. The exceptions cluster at two ends: the big family-oriented beach resorts, which charge or refuse to protect their image, and a handful of budget places that charge because they can.

Even at guest-friendly hotels there are standard conditions, and almost every written policy we have seen includes some version of them:

- **One visitor free.** The free allowance is one unregistered guest when you are booked into a double room. A second visitor at the same time, or a third person in the room, is charged, usually 500 to 1,000 THB.
- **ID at the front desk.** The visitor hands over a Thai ID card or passport at reception and gets it back on leaving. This is for the hotel's security and yours; hotels that skip it are not doing you a favour. If your visitor does not have an original ID on her, most hotels will not let her upstairs. Photocopies are not accepted.
- **Minimum age.** Between 18 and 21 depending on the hotel. Most say 20.
- **One per day at some hotels.** A few properties, Flipper Lodge among them, allow one visitor per day. Others allow more as long as they come one at a time.
- **No restriction on who.** Written policies say "visitor", and where they are specific they say women and ladyboys are treated the same. Hilton's policy states this explicitly.

## By Area

### Beach Road and Central Pattaya

The best-known guest-friendly hotels in Pattaya are on or just behind Beach Road, and this is where the luxury end of the list lives.

[**Hilton Pattaya**](/stay/hilton-pattaya/) is the one everyone asks about, because a five-star hotel above a shopping mall does not look like a place that welcomes visitors at 2am. It does. The hotel's policy is no joiner fee, ID deposited at the front desk and returned on departure, and no limit on the number of visitors over a stay. If your visitor wants breakfast at Edge, it is 550 THB. The Hilton is not a place where visitors are waved through without a glance, but the process is discreet and the staff have seen it all.

[**Holiday Inn Pattaya**](/stay/holiday-inn-pattaya-by-ihg/) on Beach Road near Soi 6 has confirmed in writing that unregistered guests are allowed without charge, ID at reception. It is a large, busy, very well-reviewed hotel with a family clientele by day and no apparent issue with what its guests do by night.

[**Hard Rock Hotel**](/stay/hard-rock-hotel/) is guest-friendly with no fee, and the pool scene makes it one of the more fun places in the city to have a visitor stay over. [**Siam@Siam**](/stay/siam-at-siam/) in North Pattaya is the design-hotel option, also no fee, with the rooftop bar as a built-in first stop. [**Avani Pattaya Resort**](/stay/avani-pattaya-resort/) is listed as guest-friendly with no fee by the directories; we have not seen the wording, so confirm when you book.

Behind Beach Road, [**AYA Boutique Hotel**](/stay/aya-boutique-hotel-pattaya/) in the City Walk alley allows one visitor free, minimum age 20, and charges 1,000 THB for a third person. [**Mera Mare**](/stay/mera-mare-pattaya/) at the north end has the same shape of policy with a minimum age of 18. [**Flipper Lodge**](/stay/flipper-lodge-hotel/), fifty metres from the beach, allows one visitor per day free, minimum age 20; that "per day" is worth noting if your plans are ambitious. [**Natural Beach Hotel**](/stay/natural-beach-hotel/) is the budget pick in this zone.

Further back towards Second Road, [**Areca Lodge**](/stay/areca-lodge-pattaya/) on Soi Diana is a long-standing favourite and has the clearest written policy of any hotel we know: maximum two people in a double room, one visitor free, 500 THB per additional person, minimum age 20, ID required. [**Hotel Baraquda Heeton**](/stay/hotel-baraquda-heeton-pattaya-by-compass-hospitality/), [**Acqua Hotel**](/stay/acqua-hotel-pattaya/) and [**Adelphi**](/stay/adelphi-pattaya/) are all confirmed no-fee on our list and sit within a few minutes' walk of each other and of Soi Buakhao.

[**The Classroom**](/stay/the-classroom-pattaya/) deserves its own line: it is guest-friendly, and it has a separate entrance that visitors can use if either of you would rather not walk through the lobby. No other hotel in Pattaya that we know of offers that.

## Hotels That Charge, or Say No

The other side of the list is just as useful.

The large family resorts in Pattaya do not appear on any verified guest-friendly list, and there is a reason. [**Centara Grand Mirage**](/stay/centara-grand-mirage-beach-resort-pattaya/), the [**Royal Cliff**](/stay/royal-cliff-beach-hotel-pattaya/) hotels, [**Cape Dara**](/stay/cape-dara-resort-pattaya/), the [**InterContinental**](/stay/intercontinental-pattaya-resort-by-ihg/) and the [**Marriott**](/stay/pattaya-marriott-resort-and-spa/) are built for families and conferences and price their rooms accordingly. Some will register a visitor for a fee, which at this level tends to be 1,000 to 1,500 THB or more, some will decline, and none of them advertise the policy. If you are booking one of these because it is a beautiful resort and you may or may not have company, email first and expect the answer to be a fee.

The same goes for the newer upscale towers in Central Pattaya, [**Grande Centre Point**](/stay/grande-centre-point-pattaya/) and [**Mytt**](/stay/mytt-hotel-pattaya/) among them. Neither is on a verified list. That does not mean no, but it means ask.

At the budget end, a small number of guesthouses charge 300 to 500 THB per visitor simply because their guests are short-stay and the fee is easy money. These are not worth naming individually; they change constantly. If a place under 800 THB a night does not say "guest-friendly" somewhere in its listing, assume a fee until you have asked.

## What Actually Happens at the Front Desk

The process at a guest-friendly hotel is the same almost everywhere, and knowing it in advance removes the awkwardness.

You arrive together. Your visitor goes to reception, hands over her Thai ID card, and the staff note her details against your room number. Sometimes she is given a visitor card or a receipt for the ID; sometimes it is simply held in a drawer. She gets the ID back when she leaves, whether that is at 3am or after breakfast. At a few hotels the ID goes in a safety box and you are given the key.

If she does not have an ID card with her, the hotel will not let her up. This is not negotiable at any hotel we know of, and it is a good rule; it means the hotel knows who is in the building. It also means you should ask before you leave the bar, not in the lobby.

Breakfast is for registered guests. Some hotels, the Hilton among them, will sell a visitor breakfast at the restaurant price. Many mid-range hotels will quietly let it go if she is with you. Do not assume either.

Nobody at a guest-friendly hotel will comment, judge or treat your visitor as anything other than a guest. That is the whole point of choosing one.

## How to Ask a Hotel Before You Book

If a hotel is not on this list, or you want to be certain, ask. Hotels in Pattaya get this question dozens of times a week and answer it plainly.

The most reliable channel is the message function on Agoda or Booking.com after you have made a booking with free cancellation, or an email to the hotel directly. Keep it to two lines:

> I am booking a double room for one person from [dates]. Do you allow an unregistered visitor to stay overnight in the room, and is there a joiner fee? Thank you.

You will get one of three answers: no fee and a note about ID; a fee and the amount; or a no. All three are useful. Phoning works too, but written answers are better if the night staff later disagree.

Do not ask on the hotel's Facebook page or in a public review. Do ask before the free-cancellation window closes.

## Booking Tips

- **Book a double, not a single.** The free-visitor allowance is tied to double occupancy. A single room with a single bed may mean a charge even at a guest-friendly hotel.
- **Set occupancy to two if the price is the same.** Some booking sites let you do this at no extra cost, and it removes any argument about a second person in the room.
- **Choose a room with a safe** and use it. Passport, spare cards, most of your cash. This is standard advice for Pattaya, not a comment on anyone in particular.
- **Walking distance matters at 2am.** A hotel five minutes from [Walking Street](/areas/walking-street/) or LK Metro is worth more than a nicer one twenty minutes away by baht bus.
- **Do not confuse "guest-friendly" with "anything goes".** These are ordinary hotels with a sensible visitor policy. Noise complaints, damage and unregistered visitors who skip the front desk are handled the way any hotel would handle them.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is a joiner fee?

A charge a hotel adds when an unregistered visitor stays in your room. In Pattaya it runs from 300 THB at some budget places to 1,000 to 1,500 THB at upscale resorts. Guest-friendly hotels do not charge one for the first visitor.

### Is Hilton Pattaya guest-friendly?

Yes. The hotel's policy is no joiner fee, with the visitor's ID held at the front desk during the stay. Breakfast for a visitor is charged at 550 THB.

### Do all hotels in Pattaya allow visitors?

Most do, particularly mid-range hotels in the nightlife areas. The large family resorts and a few budget guesthouses either charge a fee or decline. When in doubt, ask before the free-cancellation window closes.

### Does my visitor need ID?

Yes, at every hotel we know of. A Thai ID card or passport, the original, left at reception and returned on departure. Without it she will not be allowed up.

### Can I bring two visitors?

At most guest-friendly hotels the second person at the same time is charged, usually 500 to 1,000 THB. A few hotels allow visitors up to the room's maximum occupancy if you have booked a triple. Bringing two separate visitors on different nights is normally fine and free.

### Is there a minimum age for visitors?

Yes, and hotels enforce it. Between 18 and 21 depending on the property; most written policies say 20.

### Are guest-friendly hotels lower quality?

No. The list above includes the Hilton, the Hard Rock, Siam@Siam and Holiday Inn. Guest-friendly is a policy, not a category of hotel.

## Final Thoughts

The guest-friendly question is one of the few things about a Pattaya hotel that a booking site will not tell you, and one of the few that can ruin a night if you get it wrong. The list above is as honest as we can make it: confirmed where we have confirmation, flagged where we do not, and clear about the hotels that charge.

Pick a hotel from the list in the area you will actually be spending your nights, book a double, and let the front desk do its job. It is a system that has worked in this city for forty years.
