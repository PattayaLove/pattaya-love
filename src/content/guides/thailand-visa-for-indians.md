The short version: **Indian passports keep visa-free entry to Thailand.** India is on the new list of 60 eligible countries alongside the EU, UK, US, Canada, Australia, Japan and Switzerland.

What changes is how long you can stay, and what the exemption covers.

Last checked **4 September 2026**. This is not immigration advice — rules changed a week ago and change again in eleven days. Confirm anything decision-critical with the Royal Thai Embassy in New Delhi or a licensed visa agent.

## What Changes on 15 September

| | Until 14 September | From 15 September |
|---|---|---|
| Visa-free stay | 60 days | **30 days** |
| Covers | Tourism and short business visits | **Tourism only** |
| Eligible countries | 93 | 60 (India still included) |
| Land border entries | Unlimited | **Two per calendar year** |

Arrivals up to and including 14 September are processed under the old rules. The Royal Thai Embassy in New Delhi has confirmed that travellers who enter before the change keep their permitted stay — it is not shortened retroactively.

**The business visit change is the one being missed.** The outgoing 60-day exemption covered short business trips. The new 30-day exemption does not. If you are travelling for meetings, a conference or a trade fair, the visa-free route no longer covers you from 15 September, regardless of how long you stay.

**The land border cap matters for anyone doing runs.** Two overland entries per calendar year under the exemption. Arriving by air is not capped.

## What Immigration Actually Asks For

Most travellers walk through without being asked anything. Be able to produce all of it anyway, because the officer decides and there is no appeal at the desk.

- **Passport valid at least six months** beyond your arrival date.
- **Thailand Digital Arrival Card (TDAC)** — completed before you travel. This replaced the paper arrival card and is a common reason for delays among people who did not know it existed. The quickest way to file it is the **THIM app**, the Immigration Bureau's own app for iPhone and Android: point the camera at your passport, most fields fill themselves, done in about three minutes. It is free — see the warning below.
- **Confirmed return or onward ticket.** A one-way booking is the single most common cause of denied boarding, and airlines check this before immigration ever sees you.
- **Accommodation booking** for at least the first nights.
- **Proof of funds.** Sources differ on the figure: commonly quoted as 10,000 THB per person or 20,000 per family, but the requirement stated for visa-exempt entry is **20,000 THB per person or 40,000 per family**. Plan for the higher number. Cash, or a bank statement you can open on your phone.

### Why Indian travellers get asked more often

The Indian Embassy in Bangkok published an eleven-point advisory for travellers in 2026, specifically to reduce boarding denials and entry refusals. Embassies do not write those unless there is a pattern.

The practical reading: carry the documents above and have them accessible rather than buried in luggage. Being able to show a return ticket and a hotel booking in fifteen seconds is the difference between a question and a problem.

## How to Stay Longer Than 30 Days

Three routes, in ascending order of effort.

**Extension at immigration — 30 extra days.** After entering visa-free you can apply at a Thai immigration office for a 30-day extension. It costs 1,900 THB and is usually processed the same day. For Pattaya that means the office on Jomtien Soi 5 — see our [Pattaya visa guide](/guides/pattaya-visa-guide/) for opening hours, the dress code that gets people turned away, and the photocopies to bring.

**Tourist visa (TR) — 60 days.** Applied for at a Thai embassy or consulate in India before you travel, extendable by a further 30 days once you are here. If you already know you want more than a month, this is simpler than arriving and extending.

**DTV — five years.** The Destination Thailand Visa gives 180 days per entry over five years, renewable once per entry. It requires 500,000 THB in liquid funds and either proof of remote work income from outside Thailand, or enrolment in a qualifying activity such as Muay Thai training. Two requirements were added on 31 August 2026: proof of permanent residence and a criminal record certificate.

## Thirty Days Is Enough for a Holiday

For a normal trip, none of this matters. The average visit runs well under two weeks and 30 days is generous.

If you are coming to Pattaya specifically, the practical planning is elsewhere on this site: [where to stay by area](/areas/), the [Bangkok to Pattaya routes](/guides/bangkok-to-pattaya/) since most arrivals land at Suvarnabhumi, and a [Thai SIM or eSIM](/guides/thailand-sim-esim/) sorted before you need Grab.

Two things worth knowing that catch first-time visitors regardless of passport: the baht bus is 15 THB and you press the buzzer to stop it, and [travel insurance](/guides/pattaya-healthcare/) commonly excludes both alcohol-related injury and riding a motorbike without the correct licence — which together account for most of what goes wrong here.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Do Indians still get visa-free entry to Thailand?

Yes. India is among the 60 countries on the list that takes effect on 15 September 2026. The stay drops to 30 days.

### Can I still travel to Thailand for business without a visa?

Not from 15 September. The new 30-day exemption covers tourism only, where the outgoing 60-day rule also covered short business visits.

### How much money do I need to show at immigration?

Sources vary between 10,000 and 20,000 THB per person. Plan for 20,000 per person or 40,000 per family so the question cannot become a problem.

### What is the TDAC?

The Thailand Digital Arrival Card, completed online before travel. It replaced the paper form and is a frequent cause of delay for people unaware of it.

### Can I extend a visa-free stay?

Yes, by 30 days at a Thai immigration office for 1,900 THB, usually same day.

### Does the change affect me if I arrive before 15 September?

No. Entries up to and including 14 September keep the 60-day allowance for the full permitted period.
