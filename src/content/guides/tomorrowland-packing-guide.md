Most packing lists for this festival are the Belgian list with sunscreen added. That misses what actually differs: a cool night on an inland hill, a cashless site, a 20-year age check at the gate, and a country where one category of mistake is genuinely serious.

Last checked **3 September 2026**.

## December Weather, Specifically

Mid-December is the best weather Pattaya gets all year, which is presumably why the date was chosen. Warm dry days, low humidity by Thai standards, the monsoon finished, and a brief shower possible but not typical.

The part people get wrong is the night. Wisdom Valley sits inland on rolling terrain rather than on the coast, and December evenings in Thailand's cool season drop enough that standing still in damp clothes at 2am is unpleasant. You will be sweating at 8pm and cold at 3am on the same night.

**Bring one warm layer.** A light hoodie or long-sleeve you can tie round your waist. This is the single item most people leave behind and want back.

## The Packing List

**Clothing**

- Lightweight, breathable pieces — cotton or moisture-wicking, not synthetic party wear that traps heat
- One warm layer for the late sets
- More changes of clothing than you think; nothing dries fast at a festival
- Closed shoes with grip. Not sandals, not new shoes. You will be on your feet for eight hours on uneven ground
- A compact rain poncho — umbrellas are useless in a crowd and get confiscated at many events

**For the daytime stages**

- High-SPF sunscreen, and actually reapply it
- A hat and sunglasses
- A refillable water bottle
- Electrolyte sachets. Heat plus dancing plus alcohol dehydrates faster than you notice

**Practical**

- Earplugs. This is a full Tomorrowland rig, and three days of it does permanent damage without them
- A power bank, charged
- A crossbody bag or waist pack you can actually dance in
- Passport photocopy in your bag, original in the hotel safe
- Any personal medication in its original packaging

## Bring ID, Not Just Your Ticket

The minimum age is **20**, and the cutoff is your birthday rather than your birth year. Carry photo ID that proves it.

A photocopy is fine for most purposes in Thailand, but for an age check at a festival gate, bring something official. If you are 20 or 21, assume you will be asked.

Full detail on this and the pass tiers is in the [ticket guide](/guides/tomorrowland-tickets/).

## Money: Cashless Inside, Cash Outside

The festival runs on **Pearls** loaded to your bracelet. Your bracelet is your ticket and your wallet, and food, drinks and merchandise inside the site are all paid for by tapping it.

Top up before you travel rather than queuing on site, and load in stages rather than dumping your whole budget on at once — check the refund terms for unspent Pearls first.

Outside the venue is a different country entirely. Baht buses, motorbike taxis, market stalls and small restaurants in Pattaya are cash. A baht bus is 15 THB and the driver is not taking your festival bracelet.

Two practical notes:

- **ATM fees.** Foreign cards are charged a per-withdrawal fee at Thai ATMs. Take out larger amounts less often rather than small amounts daily.
- **QR payments are everywhere now.** Thailand runs on PromptPay QR far more than on cards. Our [Thai banking and QR payments guide](/guides/thai-banking-qr-payments/) explains what actually works for a visitor, and the [currency converter](/tools/currency-converter/) helps you calibrate.

## Get Connected Before You Need To

You will need mobile data for the shuttle, for Grab, for maps, for finding people in a 237-acre field, and for your bracelet top-ups.

Sort a [Thai SIM or eSIM](/guides/thailand-sim-esim/) before you arrive or on arrival, not on the festival morning. The airport counters will be busy that week and coverage inside a crowd of 50,000 is not something you want to be troubleshooting at the gate.

Agree a meeting point with your group before you go in, and a fallback for when the network is congested. It will be congested.

## The One That Is Not Negotiable

Thailand's drug laws are severe, enforcement around a major event will not be relaxed, and being at a music festival is not a mitigating circumstance. Penalties reach lengths that end lives, and a foreign passport is not protection.

There is no clever version of this paragraph. Do not.

## Health and Insurance

Three days of heat, dancing, alcohol and very little sleep puts more people in a clinic than anyone expects, and the common causes are dull: dehydration, heat exhaustion, a cut foot, a fall.

Pattaya has good private hospitals and they are not free. Check that your travel insurance actually covers you — many policies exclude injuries sustained while intoxicated, and some exclude festivals or "hazardous activities" outright. Read the exclusions rather than the summary page, and do it before you fly.

Basic self-care that people skip: drink water between drinks, eat properly at least once a day, and take a break in shade during the afternoon. Pacing across three days beats going hard on Friday and writing off Saturday.

## What to Leave at the Hotel

- Your passport. A copy is enough
- Anything valuable you would be upset to lose in a crowd
- Glass, in any form — it is prohibited at Tomorrowland events
- Large bags. Check the current bag policy on the [official FAQ](https://faq.tomorrowlandthailand.com/hc/en-us) before you pack one; the Thailand rules had not been fully published as of early September

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Do I need warm clothes for Tomorrowland Thailand?

One light layer, yes. December days are warm but the venue is inland and the small hours get genuinely cool.

### Can I pay with cash at the festival?

No. The site is cashless and runs on Pearls loaded to your bracelet. You need Thai cash for everything outside the gates.

### What ID do I need?

Photo ID proving you are 20 or older. The age minimum is 20, based on your birthday rather than your birth year.

### Is a rain poncho necessary in December?

Rain is unlikely but not impossible. A compact poncho weighs nothing and an umbrella is not usable in a festival crowd.

### Does travel insurance cover a festival?

Not always. Many policies exclude intoxication-related injury and some exclude festivals. Check the exclusions specifically before you rely on it.
